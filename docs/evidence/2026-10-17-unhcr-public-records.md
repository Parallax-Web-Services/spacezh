# UNHCR winter 2024–25: public transfer and donor records

Checked 17 October 2026. This is an AI-assisted examination of public records, not a field verification, independent audit or human editorial approval. No external enquiry was sent. This note covers a generator ownership transfer and FCDO's UNHCR funding relationship.

## One signed ownership-transfer agreement

Fastiv City Council's [resolution no. 259-LXX-VIII](https://fastiv-rada.gov.ua/pro-nadannia-zhody-ta-pryjniattia-do-komunalnoi-vlasnosti-fastivskoi-miskoi-terytorialnoi-hromady-v-osobi-fastivskoi-miskoi-rady-humanitarnoi-blahodijnoi-dopomohy-u-vyhliadi-heneratora-coelmo-50-kva-2/) is dated **10 April 2025** and was posted **16 April 2025**. Its operative paragraphs approve acceptance of one UNHCR generator into municipal ownership and approve the attached agreement dated 28 January 2025. The resolution's appendix identifies the unit of measure as pieces and the quantity as **1**.

The council publishes the signed agreement in [English](https://fastiv-rada.gov.ua/wp-content/uploads/259-dodatok.pdf) and [Ukrainian](https://fastiv-rada.gov.ua/wp-content/uploads/259-dodatok-1.pdf). These are **two language versions of the same agreement**, not evidence of two generators. Each PDF contains four scanned pages.

- **PDF p. 1:** the effective date is **28 January 2025** and the registration identifier is **KYIV_GEN_18_2025**.
- **English PDF p. 2, Article II:** UNHCR's rights in, and responsibility for, the assets cease on the agreement's effective date. Article IV refers generally to physical handover, without recording an actual delivery date.
- **English PDF p. 3, Article VIII and signature blocks:** the agreement takes effect on the first-page date; the page bears signatures and stamps, with a 28 January 2025 date in the recipient's signature block. A signed ownership-transfer agreement is stronger evidence than a prospective assistance announcement, but its effective date and the council's later decision/publication dates must not be relabelled as a verified physical delivery date.
- **PDF p. 4, Annex A:** the asset list contains one generator and states **Total: 1**. The annex belongs to this individual agreement; it is not a national distribution list.

The evidence supports a specific, signed UNHCR generator ownership-transfer record. It **does not establish whether that unit is included in either the 110 generators in UNHCR's 14 April 2025 update or the 186 generators in its July 2025 monitoring report**. It therefore cannot be assigned to **any part of the 76-unit difference** between those publications. Neither an April council decision nor an April webpage publication establishes a new delivery after the April UNHCR report. A bridge still requires the underlying aggregate delivery or inventory records and their count definitions.

## FCDO agreements and donor review

The official [HARP programme document register](https://devtracker.fcdo.gov.uk/programme/GB-GOV-1-400176/documents) lists the UNHCR memorandum and amendment as first published in **May 2025**, and the annual review as first published in **November 2025**. Those are register publication months, not the dates on which UNHCR received money or paid recipients. The register warns that some documents may be excluded for privacy or security reasons. Its other memoranda were screened by party name to identify the UNHCR documents below; this was not a review of all 38 listed documents.

### UNHCR memorandum D0007515

The [public memorandum](https://iati.fcdo.gov.uk/iati_documents/D0007515.odt), opening “Provision of grant” paragraphs, describes protection, shelter/housing and winter cash support for war-affected people in Ukraine. It sets an activity period of **15 November 2024–31 March 2025** and an original contribution ceiling of **£10 million**, subject to conditions and revision. The allocation table separately starts on 8 November; that table date must not replace the stated activity start date. The public copy's FCDO signature block is dated 14 November 2024; the partner date is redacted as “xx November 2024.” The later amendment refers to the original memorandum as signed on 20 November 2024.

**Reporting paragraph 24** requires a final narrative and financial report detailing expenditure within six months of the project's end or the memorandum's termination, plus audited accounts by the end of November 2025. The public memorandum contains administrative forms and policy annexes, rather than a completed final results report, paid-recipient ledger or cash-specific expenditure schedule. Its obligation to report is not evidence that the report was delivered or made public. No cash-only allocation was identified in this memorandum or its attached annexes. The contribution ceiling covers several activities and is not proof of the winter-cash funding received.

### Amendment D0007516

The [public amendment no. 1/2025](https://iati.fcdo.gov.uk/iati_documents/D0007516.odt), **Annex B**, raises the contribution ceiling by **£1,360,738**, from £10 million to **£11,360,738**. The FCDO date is **26 February 2025**; the partner's day is redacted as “XX February 2025.” The amendment does not provide a cash-specific allocation, a completed payment request or a recipient-results reconciliation. Its enlarged ceiling must not be described as a verified cash disbursement.

### Annual review S40017629

The [HARP annual review](https://iati.fcdo.gov.uk/iati_documents/S40017629.odt) is dated **16 November 2025**. **Section A, paragraph 3**, says it assesses October 2024–June 2025 and does not cover the whole breadth of UK humanitarian assistance during that period. The following are the UNHCR findings and relevant limits:

- **Section A, paragraph 4, partner spending table:** the UNHCR row reports **£11,360,738** as HARP 2024/25 actual spend and zero as HARP 2025/26 first-quarter actual spend. This is FCDO's programme-level spend attribution to UNHCR across activities, not a winter-cash-only figure or independent confirmation of when the recipient agency received funds.
- **Output 2, paragraph 33, indicator 2.1.1 partner/cash-type table:** UNHCR is attributed **30,339 winter-cash recipients** and **7,500 multipurpose-cash recipients**, total 37,839. The introductory sentence defines the table's unit as individuals. These are results attributed to this donor programme, not a replacement national total, a separate group that can safely be added to 258,345, or a bridge to UNHCR's original 550,000-person target.
- **Output 2, indicator 2.1.2 immediately following that table:** the review attributes the **inter-agency** winter-cash plan's 53% reach to funding shortages. This is explicit evidence of funding constraints in the wider winter response; it does **not** quantify the causes of UNHCR's separate 46.97% result. The two rates have different scopes and cannot be substituted for one another.
- **Paragraph 35:** the review describes the February 2025 UNHCR top-up as mostly for multipurpose cash and shelter/house repairs. It supplies no allocation of the top-up to winter cash.
- **Paragraph 42:** cleansing local-authority data affected winter-cash timeliness, particularly for the Ukrainian Red Cross Society under a different programme. The paragraph recommends measuring UNHCR winter-cash timeliness more effectively. It does not assign the described delay or a number of missed payments to UNHCR.
- **Paragraph 43, “Third party monitoring assessment – UNHCR”:** the review recommends earlier FCDO winterisation disbursements to improve seasonal preparedness. It discusses improving efficiency, monitoring and inclusion; it gives no cause-by-cause count of people omitted from UNHCR's original target. This is a published donor summary of third-party monitoring, not direct access to the underlying monitoring report.

These documents establish a specific donor agreement, an amendment and donor-reported spending/results. They improve the evidence about this funding relationship but do **not** reconcile UNHCR's 291,655-person winter-cash target gap. No generator-count bridge was found in them. The cash-only allocation, recipient reconciliation and completed grant narrative/financial report remain unverified in this review.

## Component budget announcements

The [Social Policy Ministry announcement of 28 October 2024](https://www.msp.gov.ua/press-center/news/minsotspolityky-ta-uvkb-oon-ob%27yednuyut-zusyllya-dlya-pidtrymky-ukrayintsiv-pid-chas-zymy), final programme-figure paragraphs, envisages UNHCR-backed support for **92,000 households** and **$30 million**, separate from state-funded assistance. The later UNHCR announcement's **57,000-household** ambition is a different dated record; no signed amendment explaining that difference was obtained.

The [state-company announcement of 28 November 2024](https://dp-reintegration.gov.ua/en/unhcr-increases-funding-for-frontline-support-project-to-40-6-million/), opening budget paragraphs, records the separate frontline component increasing **$30 million → $37 million → $40.6 million**. It describes approximately 71,000 households and an expected additional 7,000. It identifies the 21 August Ministry of Reintegration–UNHCR memorandum, but the signed memorandum was not obtained. The current publisher is the State Non-Profit Company National Agency for Territorial Development of Ukraine. This is an official programme announcement rather than a UNHCR donor ledger.

Both full public articles were read. Announced component budgets are not secured donor receipts, cash-only expenditure, final seasonal funding or a quantified explanation of the original 550,000-person target gap. They must not be summed to create a final funding total.
